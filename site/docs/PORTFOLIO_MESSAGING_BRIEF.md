## The job of the site

The portfolio should make one point quickly: Carl is an experienced product engineer who can take complicated product work from an unclear problem to an interface and system that people can use, inspect, and maintain.

Current professional work and current projects must establish that point. Historical creative-technology work should explain why Carl brings unusual visual and interaction judgment to product engineering; it should not make the homepage read like an archive or imply that old tools are his present-day specialization.

## Audience hierarchy

1. **Hiring managers and engineering leaders** need to understand Carl's current level, judgment, ownership, technical range, and the maturity of each project.
2. **Technical interviewers and potential teammates** need inspectable architecture, repositories where available, implementation boundaries, and honest limitations.
3. **Recruiters** need a clear role category, current stack vocabulary, employment history, location/contact paths, and a usable résumé.
4. **Product collaborators and founders** need evidence that Carl can translate between product, design, engineering, and delivery.
5. **Prospective venture partners** are a secondary audience. Entrepreneurial work may demonstrate initiative, but the site should not imply traction, funding, customers, or commercial readiness without evidence.

When these audiences compete, optimize the first screen and primary navigation for hiring managers. Give technical readers depth inside the work and evidence views rather than loading the hero with terminology.

## Literal positioning statement

> Carl Welch is a senior product engineer and creative technologist who builds interfaces and product systems for complicated workflows. His recent work centers on React, TypeScript, security-product administration, analytics, applied AI, and independently developed software.

This is a review statement, not approved homepage copy. It is deliberately literal. It should not be replaced with a clever slogan.

## Value proposition

Carl combines four kinds of work that are often separated:

- frontend and interface-system engineering;
- product judgment for operational and ambiguous workflows;
- testing, reusable components, documentation, and delivery discipline; and
- visual, interactive, and creative-technology experience.

The benefit is not that Carl "does everything." The benefit is that he can keep the user experience, system boundary, and delivery reality in view at the same time.

## Proof hierarchy

Use proof in this order:

1. **Recent professional work:** role, dates, product area, direct contribution, and current-stack evidence.
2. **Current portfolio systems:** inspectable implementation, repository, architecture, maturity, and limitations.
3. **Attributed recommendations:** exact wording and context, only after separate publication approval.
4. **Selected historical work:** a few records that explain creative and teaching continuity.
5. **Aspirational ventures:** clearly labeled prototypes, pre-release work, experiments, or plans.

Never use an older accolade or recommendation to compensate for missing evidence about current engineering work.

## Tone rules

The voice should be direct, experienced, visually aware, and specific.

- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs: built, implemented, migrated, tested, documented, designed, reviewed, shipped.
- Name the product surface or system before naming the technology.
- State role and maturity plainly. "I built," "I led," "I contributed," and "the team built" are not interchangeable.
- Keep limitations near the claim they qualify.
- Let architecture, screenshots, repositories, and attributed testimony carry the persuasion.
- Use first person for Carl's work and third person only in metadata or attributed recommendation summaries.
- Write sentences that still sound natural when read aloud.

Avoid:

- slogan-first headlines;
- claims such as "AI that shows its work," "make the hard parts visible," or "code with a point of view";
- stock language such as *leveraging*, *unlocking*, *seamless*, *innovative solutions*, *at the intersection of*, *passionate about*, *cutting-edge*, and *from concept to completion*;
- inflated contrasts, repeated em dashes, adjective piles, and three-part lists used only for rhythm;
- calling every project a platform, production system, or business;
- presenting frameworks, protocols, RAG, MCP, graph databases, or agents as accomplishments by themselves.

## Objection handling

### "Is Carl primarily a designer or an engineer?"

Lead with current senior product-engineering work. Use the creative-technology archive to explain his visual and interaction judgment, not to blur his role.

### "Are the AI projects production systems?"

Answer per project. Job Search OS is a protected single-user production application. Flight Tracker AI is a deployed read-only portfolio demo. Wave Factory Essentials is pre-release. Public Jolene integration is locally validated but not publicly deployed or launched.

### "His work covers many domains; what is the through-line?"

The through-line is product engineering for complex workflows, with strong interface judgment and explicit operating boundaries. Domain breadth is supporting evidence, not the headline.

### "Why include old Flash, game, or teaching work?"

Include only enough to show that interaction design, visual communication, and technical explanation are longstanding parts of Carl's practice. Do not use the archive as evidence of current tooling.

### "Is the portfolio claiming confidential employer outcomes?"

No. Keep employer descriptions at the level of public product areas and Carl's verified responsibilities. Do not expose internal product data, customer information, proprietary architecture, private correspondence, or unsupported metrics.

### "Is Carl looking for employment, clients, or investors?"

Employment and senior product-engineering conversations are primary. Product collaboration and founder conversations can remain available as secondary contact intents. Do not imply fundraising, availability, rates, relocation, or commercial traction.
